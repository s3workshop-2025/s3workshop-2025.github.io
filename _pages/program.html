    Keynote 1: Towards Sensing Intelligence with Ambient Systems

    Abstract: Recent advancements in cyber-physical systems — such as wearable devices, virtual reality, and robotics — are transforming how we live, work, and commute. These fast-emerging technologies lead to various unprecedented sensing requirements — for example, can we seamlessly track human motion on a millimeter scale? Is it possible to accurately interpret the nuances of gestures and poses? In this talk, I will first present my recent efforts in developing novel magnetic sensing systems. These systems pave pathways for achieving ultra-accurate sensing accuracy with minimum overheads, thus facilitating essential applications exemplified by at-home healthcare and sports analytics. Next, I will show how ambient sensors, including inertial measurement units (IMUs) embedded in our everyday electronics, can characterize subconscious behaviors like driving habits. Moreover, I will introduce my works on protecting user’s privacy in the era of pervasive sensing. Finally, I will conclude by outlining key challenges and future directions in next-generation sensing systems and beyond.
    Bio: Dongyao Chen is an Assistant Professor of Computer Science at Shanghai Jiao Tong University (SJTU). He received his Ph.D. in Computer Science and Engineering from the University of Michigan - Ann Arbor in 2020, advised by Prof. Kang G. Shin. His research interests lie in mobile sensing, wearable systems, and security/privacy issues of emerging sensing technologies. His research has been published in conferences such as MobiCom, MobiSys, UbiComp, SenSys, and CCS. His work received the Distinguished Paper Award at UbiComp 2025 and the Best Paper Award at VehicleSec 2023. He co-founded DriverX, a mobile telematic startup focusing on democratizing sensing systems for safe driving.

    Keynote 2: Building On-device Multimodal Sensing and AI Systems for Smart Health

    Abstract: Nowadays Internet of Things (IoT) and mobile devices are all around our daily lives, offering the capabilities of in-situ sensing and networked computing across connected devices. However, deploying AI models on resource-constrained devices and in real-world applications still faces many practical challenges. Our work aims to empower IoT and mobile devices with AI to create ambient intelligence, facilitating real-world applications, particularly in smart health. First, I will introduce how we address real-world data and system challenges in advancing multimodal AI within practical IoT systems, such as distributed and imperfect IoT data, limited resources, and system dynamics. Then, I will talk about our work on leveraging AI-powered multimodal sensor devices for in-home and community-based healthcare, including detecting behavioral biomarkers of chronic diseases, generating personalized health interventions using large language models (LLMs), and understanding behavioural and cognitive activities.
    Bio: Xiaomin Ouyang is an Assistant Professor at the Department of Computer Science & Engineering, Hong Kong University of Science and Technology. Previously, she was a postdoctoral researcher at UCLA and received her Ph.D. degree from CUHK. Her research interests include embedded AI, IoT, and smart health, with a primary focus on building AI-powered mobile and IoT systems for in-home and community-based healthcare. She received Distinguished TPC members at ACM SenSys 2025, ACM MobiSys 2023 Best Paper Award and ACM SIGBED China Outstanding Doctoral Dissertation Award. She was named one of EECS Rising Stars in 2023 and NIH mHealth Scholars in 2024.
